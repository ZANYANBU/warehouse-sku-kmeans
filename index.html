    How K-Means works

    1. Pick k centresAt random.
    2. Measure distanceFrom each SKU to every centre.
    3. AssignEach SKU joins its nearest centre.
    4. Update centresMove each to the mean of its SKUs.
    5. RepeatUntil no SKU changes cluster.
    About the numbers
    • The real-data results use Olist's public e-commerce dataset (MIT licence): the 383 products ordered at least 30 times. It has no handling field, so weight stands in for handling needs.
    • The demo below it uses example data made up in your browser. Its product names and pictures are examples.
    • Clusters use all four numbers: picks per week, season index, volume and a fragile flag. The picture shows two at a time.
    • Error is the within-cluster variance on scaled data, the best of 10 runs for each k.
    • The first start for each k is picked to show the steps clearly. New starting centres is a plain random start, and can get stuck in a worse grouping.
    • Walk per pick is a simple model: one 40 m aisle from the packing desk, the most-picked group nearest, each group taking as much aisle as it has products. With no grouping a product is, on average, half-way down.
    • Inside a rack, light products are on the top shelves and big ones at the bottom.
